EnterpriseOS Phase 1 and Phase 2 Implementation Review
Reviewed 11 November 2026

Review outcome

Focus the initial build on secure company onboarding and administration (Phase 1),
then workforce and project operations (Phase 2). The supplied pack describes a
substantial application, not just dashboard mockups. It is suitable for preparing
the implementation design, but policy details, action permissions and several
conflicting scope choices still need a recorded baseline.

Three parallel reviews covered scope, backend/data/security and frontend/UI.
All findings below are review recommendations unless explicitly identified as a
requirement from a source. No client approval is implied. Original files remain
unchanged. No application implementation was performed during this review.

1. Sources and coverage

Read all seven DOCX documents and checked extracted content from their seven
PDF counterparts: Complete Project Documentation, Master Feature Catalog, and
Phase 1 through Phase 5 Client Requirements BRD/SRS. Also reviewed all 316 rows
of EnterpriseOS_Feature_Backlog.csv, Phase_Document_Index.csv,
README_START_HERE.txt and all 12 JPG reference images.

PDF page counts: Complete 21; Catalog 11; Phase 1 8; Phases 2, 3, 4 and 5 each 7.
PDF extraction contains running headers, table-cell ordering and some symbol
encoding artifacts. DOCX text was used to resolve those extraction ambiguities.
This was a requirements/content and reference-image review, not a page-by-page
visual certification of PDF/Word layout.

At review time the root contained requirements and references, with no supplied
frontend/backend application or existing automated application test suite.

2. Recommended document interpretation

README, phase index and the five phase BRD/SRS documents share a five-phase plan:
1 Foundation, company onboarding, identity and RBAC.
2 HR, attendance/shifts, leave and project operations.
3 CRM, inventory/procurement, expenses/invoices and payroll.
4 Generic workflows, collaboration, documents, advanced reports and integrations.
5 Commercial subscriptions, broader hardening, operations and handover.

Complete Project Documentation section 15 instead uses stages 0 through 8. Do
not equate its phase 2 with the new Phase 2. Its architecture remains a reference;
its older phase numbering and illustrative estimates should not drive this plan.

Recommended working precedence: explicit user scope, then reconciled Phase 1/2
BRDs; catalog/backlog as candidate inventory; complete blueprint as architecture
reference. The documents do not explicitly establish precedence, so this is a
planning recommendation, not an approved document-control rule.

3. Inventory versus implementation commitments

4. Phase 1 implementation baseline

Sources: Phase 1 sections 4-8 and 11-13; FR-01 through FR-12.

Platform administrator:
- Protected platform sign-in/area, tenant directory and tenant creation.
- Unique company slug; owner provisioning and invitation.
- Tenant detail, activation, suspension/reactivation and activity overview.

Organization administrator:
- Company profile, contact/address, timezone and validated logo handling.
- User directory with search/filter/pagination; invitations and user status.
- Predefined roles, permission mapping and role assignment.
- Dashboard showing active users, pending invitations and recent activities.
- Tenant-scoped audit history.

Identity and member experience:
- Login/logout, session/refresh lifecycle, forgot/reset password.
- Expiring single-use invitation acceptance and password setup.
- Personal profile/change password, permitted navigation and dashboard shell.
- Loading/empty/error states, access denied, session expiry and responsive forms.

Security is part of this phase: trusted tenant context, authorization on every
protected operation, active membership checks, revoked/suspended access rejection,
last-admin protection, audit, hashed tokens and server-side validation.
Custom role builder (FR-13) and mandatory MFA/SSO (FR-14) remain optional choices.

5. Phase 2 implementation baseline

Sources: Phase 2 sections 4-10 and 12-13.

Organization and HR, P2-FR-0101 through 0105:
- Branches, departments, teams, designations and reporting hierarchy.
- Employee directory/profiles, employee codes and manager assignments.
- Effective-dated onboarding, transfers, position changes and offboarding.
- Employee self-service and emergency contacts.
- Field-level confidential HR access, including API/search/export behavior.

Attendance and shifts, P2-FR-0201 through 0205:
- Locations, workdays, holidays, shift templates and effective-dated rosters.
- Manual check-in/out with source and timestamps.
- Daily attendance, lateness, informational overtime and exception calculation.
- Correction request and independent supervisor review, preserving originals.

Leave, P2-FR-0301 through 0305:
- Leave types/policies, accrual, entitlement, carry-forward and balance history.
- Submit/cancel/track requests with policy-aware overlap/entitlement checks.
- Configured manager/HR approval chains, delegation and escalation.
- Atomic balance updates and auditable cancellation/reversal.

Projects, P2-FR-0401 through 0405:
- Projects, team membership, scoped visibility and archive/read-only behavior.
- Milestones, backlog, sprints and Kanban board.
- Tasks/subtasks, priority/labels, assignees, dependencies and comments.
- Activity, overdue tasks and workload; manual time entries are optional.
- Reject inactive assignees and cyclic dependencies.

Phase 2 release depends on accepted and regression-tested Phase 1 (section 2).
Payroll, CRM, invoices, inventory, biometric/GPS integration and the generic
workflow designer are outside the current phase focus.

6. Conflicts and proposed treatment

R01 Registration/domain verification
Catalog F-101-01/02 says Must. Phase 1 FR-02 specifies platform provisioning and
section 16 leaves registration mode undecided. Proposal: begin with administrator
provisioning/invitations; record public signup and domain verification separately.

R02 MFA and role customization
F-102-05 says TOTP Must; Phase 1 FR-14 and section 11 defer mandatory MFA/SSO.
F-103-06 suggests custom roles; FR-13 treats a role builder as optional.
Proposal: predefined roles and password login baseline; explicitly decide additions.

R03 Organization and imports
F-104-05 places departments in Phase 1, while P2-FR-0101 owns organization structure.
Use one shared organization module and avoid duplicated department tables/screens.
F-104-02 bulk user import is Must in catalog but absent from detailed Phase 1 FRs.
Distinguish user-account import from employee/history import; specify formats first.

R04 Mandatory controls with weak catalog priorities
F-103-09 API authorization Should, F-103-12 least privilege Could, F-102-14 access
revocation Could and F-104-14 dashboard Could conflict with Phase 1 required behavior.
F-205-15 confidential HR access Could conflicts with P2-FR-0105/UAT-P2-02.
Implement these BRD controls with their corresponding feature, regardless of label.

R05 Phase 2 feature priority mismatch
F-207-06 approval chains and F-208-08 dependencies are Should but included in
P2-FR-0304/0403. Sprint/backlog is in P2-FR-0402 while discovery still asks whether
the client needs it. Keep all listed items visible in planning; record any deferral.

R06 Identity and membership
Complete sections 2/8 assume multiple memberships and globally unique email.
Phase 1 section 5 leaves these choices open. Keep users separate from memberships;
do not expose tenant switching without an agreed policy. Membership deactivation
must not accidentally disable a global identity in an unrelated organization.

R07 Suspension
Complete sections 4.1/4.14 describe write blocking/read-only behavior. Phase 1 FR-03,
section 5 and UAT-06 deny protected access. Proposal: administrative suspension
blocks protected access; model future subscription grace/read-only separately.

R08 API naming and missing operations
Phase BRDs use /api/v1/org/...; Complete section 9 and Catalog section 9 differ.
Proposal: standardize /api/v1/auth, /api/v1/platform and /api/v1/org before UI wiring.
Route inventories are illustrative: add explicit contracts for check-out, rosters,
leave balances/cancellation, employee lifecycle, sprint/milestone and task dependency
operations. Define DTOs, authorization, error codes, pagination and conflict rules.

R09 Estimates and service objectives
Complete sections 6/15 contain illustrative concurrency, availability, RPO/RTO and
effort estimates. Phase BRDs leave actual workload, timeline and cost TBD.
Do not turn reference estimates into commitments.

7. Shared dependencies required during Phase 1/2

- Transactional invitation/reset email and retries must work in Phase 1. A small
  outbox/email worker can support this before the Phase 4 notification center.
- Phase 2 uses configured approval chains (section 12). Implement narrow leave
  and attendance decision/history components now; defer the generic designer.
- Company logos need validated file storage now. If HR/task/leave attachments are
  selected, provide minimal tenant/object authorization and private storage; the
  full document center, versioning and advanced sharing remain Phase 4.
- Migrations, CI, validation, audit, tenant tests, logs and recovery checks start
  with Phase 1. Phase 5 is broader hardening, not the first security/testing pass.
- Preserve effective-dated, auditable attendance/leave inputs for Phase 3 payroll.
  Salary calculation, payslips and statutory rules remain later work.

8. Data and business-rule design work

Use the supplied stack: React + TypeScript, Java Spring Boot, MySQL 8.4 LTS and
a modular monolith. Exact supported dependency versions should be verified when
implementation starts. Microservices, RabbitMQ and Kubernetes are not required
by Phase 1/2. Use versioned Flyway or Liquibase migrations.

Initial entities: tenants, users, memberships, roles/permissions/assignments,
invitations, sessions, password resets, organization profiles and audit events.
Add branches/departments/teams/designations, employees and effective-dated job
history; shifts/assignments, attendance events/daily summaries/adjustments;
leave policies, balances, append-only ledger, requests and approval decisions;
projects/members/milestones/sprints/tasks/dependencies/comments/time entries.

The Phase 2 table inventory is incomplete relative to its requirements: teams,
milestones, checklist records and an explicit leave ledger need design decisions.
Employee employment status, identity status and tenant-membership status are
different concepts. Model optional employee login accounts deliberately.

All tenant-owned relationships need tenant-safe constraints and service checks.
Use UTC instants plus timezone/shift-derived work dates; do not equate UTC date
with a local workday. Preserve original attendance events and correction history.
Version policies/assignments that affect historical calculations.

Resolve worked examples for overnight shifts, missing punches, overtime/grace,
weekends, half-day/hourly leave, accrual, carry-forward, pending reservations and
approved cancellation. Serialize/idempotently process competing leave decisions.
Detect task cycles safely under concurrent updates; enforce archived-project guards.

Every table/form/board needs loading, empty, error and denied states. Provide
keyboard alternatives for board moves, visible focus and responsive layouts.
Clear tenant-specific UI cache on context changes if tenant switching is enabled.

10. Recommended build sequence and proof

1 Repository/application skeleton, local environment, migrations, CI and contracts.
2 Identity/session lifecycle, trusted tenant context, permissions and audit.
3 Tenant creation/owner invitation, organization/users/roles and dashboard.
4 Phase 1 checks: duplicate slug, token expiry/reuse, permission denial, tenant swap,
  deactivation/suspension with old sessions, last-admin protection and restore drill.
5 Organization master data, HR lifecycle and confidential-field enforcement.
6 Shifts/attendance events, daily calculations, corrections and supervisor review.
7 Leave policy examples, ledger, configured approvals and cancellation/reversal.
8 Projects/members/milestones/sprints, Kanban/tasks, safe dependencies and activity.
9 Integrated regression, UAT evidence and deployment/handover for the focused scope.

Critical Phase 2 proof: cross-tenant and private-field denial; original attendance
preserved; concurrent leave decisions apply one balance change; reversals reconcile;
inactive task assignees rejected; cycles rejected; archived projects read-only;
employees cannot see unauthorized projects. Use real MySQL integration tests for
transactions/constraints, frontend checks for critical forms, and end-to-end tests
for the tenant onboarding and workforce/project journeys.

11. Decisions to capture before dependent feature implementation

- Registration mode, membership/email uniqueness, suspension and MFA policies.
- Action-level roles: self/team/branch/tenant visibility and approve/export powers.
- Employee required/private fields, account links, imports and retention.
- Attendance/leave policies and worked examples, approval delegation/escalation.
- Sprint versus Kanban scope, task states/visibility and optional time tracking.
- Upload selection/limits, sender provider, hosting/region, workload and branding.

These decisions do not prevent preparing the repository, shared architecture,
wireframes, ERD and draft API contracts. Keep proposed defaults visible and resolve
policy-dependent behavior before treating it as an accepted implementation rule.
